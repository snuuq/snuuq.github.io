Multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) is a sample-efficient framework for expensive black-box problems with multiple competing objectives, where each evaluation may require costly simulations or experiments. We begin with the basic geometry of Pareto dominance, Pareto sets, Pareto fronts, and the hypervolume indicator, then explain how Gaussian-process surrogate models and acquisition functions guide the search under a limited evaluation budget. The first half of the talk focuses on Expected Hypervolume Improvement (EHVI), one of the most widely used acquisition functions in MOBO, and its variants based on efficient EHVI computation, EHVI gradients, and analytic formulations of EHVI and qEHVI.

The core of the talk is *Expensive Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization Based on Diffusion Models*. We first discuss why EHVI-based methods are principled but practically limited: they depend on hypervolume computation, become harder to optimize as the number of objectives or non-dominated points grows, and do not directly model the distribution of Pareto-optimal solutions. We then unpack CDM-PSL, a composite diffusion-model-based Pareto set learning method that combines unconditional and conditional sample generation, guided denoising, Gaussian-process surrogate modeling, and entropy-based objective weighting. We close with how diffusion models may complement classical EHVI-centered MOBO by generating diverse, high-quality candidate solutions in expensive evaluation regimes, and where this new paradigm may still face challenges in scalability, stability, and theoretical understanding.

See [Expensive Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization Based on Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08674) for details.
